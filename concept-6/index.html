[ § 0.1 ]

What an eConsult changes.

For most concerns, your patient does not need a dermatologist in the room — they need a dermatologist's judgment. The specialist network closes that gap in hours, not weeks.

  1. [ 01 / 04 ]

    Avoid seventy percent of specialist referrals.

    Most rashes, lesions, and chronic skin presentations can be managed at the primary level once a specialist has weighed in. eConsults convert referrals into confidence — and back into your visits.

  2. [ 02 / 04 ]

    Save the patient weeks of waiting.

    Median time-to-dermatologist in most regions is six to twelve weeks. The network responds in under twenty-four hours, with treatment recommendations you can act on the same day.

  3. [ 03 / 04 ]

    Keep the primary relationship intact.

    The specialist advises; you remain the treating physician. Continuity stays with your practice — and so does the patient.

  4. [ 04 / 04 ]

    Raise the floor on difficult cases.

    The diagnoses you'd refer reflexively — atypical rashes, pigmented lesions, recalcitrant disease — get a second set of trained eyes before treatment begins.

The method.

Three movements. Designed by physicians, for physicians.

STEP 01 ~ 5 min

Submit the case.

Photos, relevant history, the question at hand. The intake is structured by board-certified dermatologists so nothing essential is missed.

  • Photographs · clinical, mid-range, dermoscopy
  • Treatment history, comorbidities, medications
  • The specific clinical question
◇
STEP 02 T+ 19h median

A dermatologist reviews.

Cases route to the right subspecialist — medical, surgical, pediatric, or dermatopathology — based on the question. Every reviewer is board-certified and MOC-compliant.

  • Routing by subspecialty + clinical question
  • One physician, accountable end-to-end
  • Audit trail preserved for the chart
◇
STEP 03 Within 24h

Receive an assessment.

Provisional diagnosis, recommended workup, treatment plan, and a clear answer to whether in-person referral is warranted. Written in language for the chart.

  • Provisional diagnosis · differential
  • Treatment recommendations · dose, duration
  • Refer-or-not guidance with rationale

━━━ PROVISIONAL DIAGNOSIS ━━━

Subacute cutaneous lupus erythematosus (SCLE), annular variant.

DIFFERENTIAL
Tinea corporis · granuloma annulare · erythema annulare centrifugum
CONFIDENCE
High — pending serology

━━━ RECOMMENDED WORKUP ━━━

  • ANA with reflex titer and pattern
  • Anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB
  • CBC with differential, complete metabolic panel
  • Consider 4mm punch biopsy if serology equivocal

━━━ TREATMENT PLAN ━━━

First-line, primary care managed.

  • Strict photoprotection · SPF 50+ daily, UPF clothing
  • Triamcinolone 0.1% ointment BID to active lesions, 2 weeks
  • Hydroxychloroquine 200mg BID with ophthalmologic baseline if persistent at 4 weeks

━━━ REFERRAL GUIDANCE ━━━

In-person dermatology referral not required at this time.

Re-consult or refer if no improvement at 6 weeks, new systemic symptoms, or biopsy considered. Rheumatology referral if serology positive.

Sarah Chen, MD Board-Certified · Medical Dermatology Stanford School of Medicine, FAAD
