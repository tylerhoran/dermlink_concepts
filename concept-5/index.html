I SPECIFICATIONS

What an eConsult changes.

For most concerns, your patient does not need a dermatologist in the room — they need a dermatologist's judgment. The specialist network closes that gap in hours, not weeks.

001 Specialist referrals avoided 70% Most rashes, lesions, and chronic skin presentations can be managed at the primary level once a specialist has weighed in. eConsults convert referrals into confidence — and back into your visits.
002 Time saved per case 6–12wk Median time-to-dermatologist in most regions is six to twelve weeks. The network responds in under twenty-four hours, with treatment recommendations you can act on the same day.
003 Primary relationship retained 100% The specialist advises; you remain the treating physician. Continuity stays with your practice — and so does the patient.
004 Difficult-case escalation ∞ The diagnoses you'd refer reflexively — atypical rashes, pigmented lesions, recalcitrant disease — get a second set of trained eyes before treatment begins.
II METHOD

Three movements.

From submission to assessment in under a day. Designed by physicians, for physicians.

  1. I Submission

    Submit the case.

    Photos, relevant history, the question at hand. The intake is structured by board-certified dermatologists so nothing essential is missed.

    • Three photographs · clinical, mid-range, dermoscopy
    • Past treatment, comorbidities, current medications
    • The specific clinical question you need answered
  2. II Review

    A dermatologist reviews.

    Cases route to the right subspecialist — medical, surgical, pediatric, or dermatopathology — based on the question. Every reviewer is board-certified and MOC-compliant.

    • Routing by subspecialty and clinical question
    • One physician, accountable end-to-end
    • Audit trail preserved for the chart
  3. III Assessment

    Receive a structured assessment.

    Provisional diagnosis, recommended workup, treatment plan, and a clear answer to whether in-person referral is warranted. Written in language for the chart.

    • Provisional diagnosis · differential
    • Treatment recommendations · medication, dose, duration
    • Refer-or-not guidance with rationale
